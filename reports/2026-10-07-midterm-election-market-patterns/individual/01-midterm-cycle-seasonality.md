# Midterm-year seasonality and post-midterm returns

**Bundle**: 01 — midterm-cycle-seasonality
**Parent question**: Is there a pattern in how the stock market goes before and after US midterm elections (incl. both chambers flipping against the president's party)? Does it spike until the election then drop, or depend on outcome?

**Mode**: quick (about 6 search/fetch operations). Survey depth. Several figures come from search-result summaries of secondary sources, not full-text reads; they are flagged. Different providers use different start years (1931, 1950, 1954, 1961, 1970), so numbers differ slightly.

## Questions addressed
- S&P 500 return and max drawdown in midterm years vs other cycle years; Q3 trough / Q4 rally.
- Returns 3/6/12 months after midterms; Sep-Oct low to year-end; sample size and dispersion.
- Pre-election vs post-election returns; reliability and confounders.

## Findings

### 1. Midterm years are the weakest and most volatile year of the cycle
- Average price return since 1931: 4.7% in midterm years vs 9.5% in all other years; median return standard deviation since 1970 is about 16% vs about 13% in other years (LPL Research, via search summary, accessed 2026-10-07; [LPL](https://www.lpl.com/research/blog/do-midterm-elections-matter-for-markets-a-historical-perspective.html), page itself returned 403, so unverified at source).
- By cycle year (Nasdaq Dorsey Wright, 2026-08-25): Year 1 +7.02%, Year 2 (midterm) +3.68%, Year 3 +13.96%, Year 4 +8.12%. Sample is 25 midterm years from 1930 ([Dorsey Wright](https://dorseywright.nasdaq.com/research/bigwire/2026/08/25/08-25-2026/midterm-election-year-update)). Only 8 of 24 completed midterm years were up in both halves.
- Drawdowns (search-summary of secondary sources, medium-low conviction): average max intra-year drawdown is about -17.8% in midterm years vs -13.2% in non-midterm years; 1961-2024 quarterly-average data show about -19.4% for Year 2. The avg max drawdown from Jan 1 to election day is about -16.2% (1950-2024). Primary source for these not located; see [search result: SentimentTrader / First Trust kit](https://www.ftportfolios.com/Commentary/Insights/2026/7/6/midterm-election-client-resource-kit---june-2026) (PDF unreadable to the fetch tool).
- Median is likely friendlier than the mean on drawdowns; not found.

### 2. Q3 is the soft spot, Q4 is the turn
- Average quarterly returns across midterm years: Q1 -0.5%, Q2 -0.6%, Q3 -0.1%, Q4 +6.6% (search summary of secondary source; medium conviction).
- Share of midterm years with positive quarter: Q1 44%, Q2 52%, Q3 58.3%, Q4 83.3% ([Dorsey Wright, 2026-08-25](https://dorseywright.nasdaq.com/research/bigwire/2026/08/25/08-25-2026/midterm-election-year-update)). Five straight weakest quarters run from Q3 of Year 1 to Q3 of Year 2, averaging under 1%.
- Second half of a midterm year was positive in 15 of 24 (62.5%), double-digit in 10 of them (same source). So the H2 rally is a tendency, not a rule: 9 of 24 were down.
- Other claim (secondary, unverified): average Q4 gain +7.8%, up about 90% of the time; when S&P was down YTD at end of Q3 of a midterm year, it gained 28.2% on average over the next 5 quarters with no losses since 1946 (search summary, [Stock Trader's Almanac / MoneyShow results](https://moneyshow.com/articles/guru-33630)). Treat the 90% and "no losses" figures with caution; they conflict with Dorsey's 83.3%.

### 3. After the midterm: strong and unusually consistent
- 12 months after: positive in all 18 post-midterm periods since 1954, average +18.2% (LPL, via [search summary](https://www.lpl.com/research/blog/do-midterm-elections-matter-for-markets-a-historical-perspective.html)). Another source puts the average at +18.8% since 1950 (secondary). Both ~18-19%.
- 6 months after: +14.1% average since 1970 (LPL, same caveat).
- 3 months after: not found in the sources reached. Unknown.
- Dispersion by year: not retrieved (no per-year table). Gap to fill.
- Sep-Oct low to year-end: only the Q3-down-YTD stat above (28.2% over 5 quarters) was found; a clean "Sep/Oct low to Dec 31" stat was not retrieved.
- Starting-point effect: years starting within 5% of a high rose a median 12.4%; years starting after a deeper fall averaged 17.5% (search summary, source not opened).

### 4. Pre- vs post-election, and reliability
- Pre-election 3-month vs post-election comparison: not found directly. Implied pattern: weak/choppy Q3, strongest Q4 and the following year. That fits "the low often arrives before the election (Sep-Oct)," not "spike into the election, then drop." The historical average runs the opposite way to the user's premise: post-election has been stronger.
- Reliability caveats:
  - Small n: about 18-25 midterm observations (1946/1950 onward), overlapping with other effects. A 100% hit rate on n=18 is notable but is not independent evidence.
  - Confounders: the post-WWII sample includes several recession and bear-market lows that happened to land in midterm years (e.g., 1962, 1974, 1982, 2002, 2022 were lows near Oct, which is partly selection; I did not verify per-year from a source). Fed policy and valuation starting points dominate. Mid-cycle drawdown is a statistical feature of Year 2 (weakest in 5-quarter stretch).
  - Outcome dependence (House/Senate flip, party): not covered in this bundle; the ETF Trends result ("divided Congress bodes well") is a lead for another bundle, not read here.
  - Averages are not forecasts; one provider explicitly warns about that.
- Conviction: tendency high-ish on direction (weak Q3, strong post-election 12m); low on any precise number or on its causal claim ("policy uncertainty fades" is LPL's narrative, not tested).
