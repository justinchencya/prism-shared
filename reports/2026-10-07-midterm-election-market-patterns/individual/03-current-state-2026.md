## Findings

### 1. Control and odds
- Control: Republicans hold the White House and "narrow majorities in both chambers" ([BNN Bloomberg, 2026-09-03](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/investing/opinion/2026/09/03/us-midterm-elections-set-to-bring-a-buying-opportunity-brooke-thackray/)). User's belief confirmed (medium-high; one source, no seat counts retrieved).
- Prediction markets (aggregator summary, undated in snippet; treat as roughly current, check live) ([DeFiRate](https://defirate.com/news/democrats-85-percent-to-flip-house-2026-midterms-senate-still-toss-up/), [SimpleFunctions](https://simplefunctions.dev/odds/election-2026.md)):
  - House flips to Democrats: Kalshi 84%, Polymarket 85%.
  - Senate: Republicans keep it ~51% (Kalshi) / ~53% (Polymarket), i.e. a toss-up.
  - Balance of power: Democratic sweep ~48-49%; D House + R Senate ~36-37%.
- So the user's "both chambers flip" scenario is roughly a coin flip (~48-49%) per markets; the modal House outcome is a flip. Caveat: thin volume ($1-7M per market), so odds are noisy. Conviction medium.

### 3. Seasonality attribution, 2026 path vs typical, risks
- Analysts frame it as typical pattern, not as cause of the rally. Typical midterm path: weak Aug-end to early Oct (median -1.4%, UBS), bottom roughly a month before election, then rally ([BNN, Thackray](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/investing/opinion/2026/09/03/us-midterm-elections-set-to-bring-a-buying-opportunity-brooke-thackray/); [Fortune/UBS](https://fortune.com/2026/09/23/in-midterm-years-october-usually-best-month-for-stocks-gains-78-9-of-the-time)).
- Stats cited: midterm-year S&P positive 38% of Septembers, 77% of Octobers (1974-2022, Thackray); October avg +3%, positive 73.7%; November +2.7%, positive 78.9%; September avg -0.8% (Carson/Detrick, since 1950); Sep-through-year-end avg ~6% vs ~4% other years (UBS); Oct 31 of midterm year to Oct 31 next: up in all 19 cases since 1950, +16.6% avg vs 6.9% other years; 12 months post-midterm +14.5% avg, 95% success (Fidelity via Fool). Sample sizes are ~19-20 midterms; the stat is overlapping with the generic fact that equities rise most years.
- 2026 deviates: strong YTD (+13%) is atypical for a midterm year, where the first nine months are usually weak. Counter-reading cited: starting near a record implies a gain closer to the near-high-year median of 12.4%, with a 10% drawdown "more likely than not" along the way (search snippet summarising a 2026 source; original not opened — low-medium conviction). Fool notes post-election optimism may already be priced in, or AI is driving gains independent of the cycle.
- JPMorgan: fundamentals (policy, growth, labor, earnings, valuation) matter more than the election calendar (via Fortune above).
- Wilson (Morgan Stanley) pro-rally-on-GOP-win quote found is from 2022; not 2026 evidence.
- Named 2026 risks: further Fed hikes / higher yields; oil and Middle East; election outcome (Democratic sweep ~48%, implications for policy/tariffs not researched here). No source found on tariffs/AI-bubble risk specifically.
