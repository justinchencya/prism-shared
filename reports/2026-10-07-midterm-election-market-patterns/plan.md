## Meta-framing
1. The "midterm-year pattern" (weak H1/Q3 then strong Q4 into/after election) is a robust, tradable seasonal, not a data-mined artifact.
2. Market reaction depends on resulting gridlock/policy-uncertainty resolution, not on which party wins; i.e. post-election relief rally occurs regardless of outcome.
3. The current rally is better explained by macro/earnings/Fed drivers than by election seasonality, so base rates should be a prior only.

## Question tree
L1-A Historical midterm-cycle pattern
 - S&P 500 return/drawdown in midterm years vs other cycle years; Q3 trough and Q4 rally; intra-year max drawdown (Stock Trader's Almanac, LPL, Ned Davis, Fidelity, Schwab data)
 - Returns 6/12 months after midterm elections; Sep-Oct low -> Nov 3 -> year-end stats; post-WWII sample size and dispersion
 - Pre-election 3-month and post-election 3/6/12-month returns split by year
L1-B Outcome dependence / divided government
 - Returns by president-party loss vs gain of seats; cases where House and/or Senate flipped (1946, 1954, 1994, 2006, 2010, 2018, 2022 etc.): market response after
 - Returns under unified vs divided government; research on gridlock and markets
 - Counter-evidence: confounders (recessions, Fed, 1974, 2000-02, 2022)
L1-C Current state check (as of 2026-10-07)
 - Verify current partisan control of Presidency/House/Senate and forecasts/odds for Nov 3, 2026 (House flip, Senate flip, both)
 - Recent S&P/Nasdaq moves late Sep-early Oct 2026 and stated drivers; YTD, valuation, Fed
 - Whether commentators attribute the rally to the midterm pattern; how current year compares to historical midterm paths
