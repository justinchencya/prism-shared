# Question
Quick-mode scan of The Mosaic Company (MOS): what is the multi-year case, and is it a buy at ~$20.5? Critiques were skipped by design (quick mode).

# Executive answer
MOS is a levered call on phosphate normalization sitting on top of a strong potash business. Potash is guided at $270-290/t and covers most of the profit [01][02], while phosphate lost $104M in Q2 on sulfur/ammonia costs [03]. Net debt of about $5.6B against a $6.5B market cap makes the equity highly sensitive to EBITDA $TICKER (tickers/ticker-scan.md). Thesis is Inconclusive and the market verdict is Hold: much of the bear case looks priced in at 7.3x EV/EBITDA, but normalization timing is exogenous and dividend safety is not established. Evidence is survey-depth and several figures come from search snippets.

# Meta-trend 1: Nutrient prices are set by supply policy and geopolitics, so MOS is a cyclical near a high-price point

Potash is strong: Q3 guide $270-290/t, tight North American inventories, record China imports [01]. Supply is the swing factor. BHP Jansen Stage 1 (about 4.2 Mtpa, ~5% of supply) starts mid-2027, while Stage 2 slipped to FY31 [01]. The Stage 2 capex ($6.9B in [01]) versus ~$8.4B in [03] was reconciled by the ticker scan: $8.4B appears to be the Stage 1 cost after a US$2.3B charge, so [03] mislabeled it (medium confidence, search snippets) $MOS. Phosphate prices are high (DAP $820-840/t) but driven by China export curbs and the Hormuz-related input shock, both of which can reverse quickly [01][02]. Global phosphate supply is being cut by cost rather than discipline [02].

Framing M1 (supply discipline sets prices, returns hinge on supply additions): **Supported.** Evidence [01][03]. Mosaic's potash cash cost ($84/t) is above Nutrien's guide (<$60/t) and Jansen is lower-cost, so MOS is a price-taker on the way down [03].

## Investment thesis 1.1: Firm potash through the Jansen ramp funds the business while phosphate normalizes
Needs potash at or above ~$250/t mine-gate through 2027-29 and sulfur/ammonia to normalize in 2027. Killed by a potash break below $250/t (Jansen plus Russia/Belarus sanctions thaw) or phosphate margins staying negative into 2027 [01][03].

### $MOS - Hold `[NEW]`

- **Company snapshot**: Largest US phosphate producer and a top-3 North American potash miner with a Brazil distribution/mining unit (Fertilizantes); Q2-26 revenue $2.8B, TTM $12.2B, market cap $6.5B $MOS.
- **Why this ticker is in this report**: It is the one-name listed expression of the potash/phosphate cycle the question probes; both meta-framings bear on it.
- **Thesis verdict**: Inconclusive. Potash support is documented, but phosphate recovery timing is exogenous and unknowable $MOS.
- **Market verdict**: Hold. Price $20.46, 7.3x TTM EV/EBITDA, near the 52-week low and at the JPMorgan target ($19) vs consensus ~$27 $MOS. The thesis is not strong enough to justify a full position given leverage; a small starter is defensible, with staged adds.
- Net debt ~$5.57B (total debt $5.86B, cash $0.29B at 2026-06-30); $2.5B revolver undrawn, covenants in compliance; net debt/EBITDA ~3.3x on 2026E, ~2.5x on 2027E (derived, low conviction on denominators) $MOS.
- Equity sensitivity: each 1x EV/EBITDA turn on ~$2.2B EBITDA is about a third of market cap; it cuts both ways $MOS.
- Q2 FCF was -$153M against a ~$0.88/yr dividend (~4.3% yield); buybacks look inactive; dividend safety is the key tail $MOS [02].
- Falsifiers: phosphate stripping margin negative into 2027; potash below ~$250/t; leverage above 3x with a dividend cut or equity raise $MOS.

# Meta-trend 2: Mosaic's self-help is real but currently unmeasurable

Brazil restructuring (Araxá divestiture, Patrocínio idled, ~$442M Q1 charge, ~$70-80M/yr opex savings), Carlsbad sale and a Vale option (>$1B) are credible pruning moves [02][03]. But phosphate production is curtailed (Faustina idled, Bartow ~40%), so the production-rate metric that would prove operational recovery cannot be observed [02][03]. The Q2 loss is dominated by exogenous input costs [02]. Management's operational-target history was not sourced.

Framing M2 (fixable self-help, normalized earnings underpriced): **Unchanged / Inconclusive.** Valuation looks undemanding on 2027E EBITDA (EV ~5.5x), but a mid-cycle estimate could not be sourced, and current potash prices flatter the base [01] $MOS.

## Implication 2.1: Re-rating requires evidence, not price alone
The evidence that would move this to Buy is a positive phosphate stripping margin in Q4-26/Q1-27 or net debt/EBITDA falling toward ~2.5x $MOS.

# Cross-cutting tensions
- **Cheap vs levered**: low multiples and an oversold chart (RSI ~30, -40% over 12 months) $MOS versus negative FCF and ~$5.6B net debt [02]. Director's call: leverage dominates; stage entry rather than average in at once.
- **Potash strength vs share price weakness**: potash earned $195M operating profit in Q2 [03], yet the stock is near its low. Market is pricing phosphate and balance-sheet risk, which is consistent with the evidence.
- **Sulfur contract below spot** (Q3 at ~$705/LT, bull point [03]) versus the "uneconomic spot" statement and Hormuz duration risk [02]. Duration is unknowable; neither side resolved.
- **Consensus basis conflict**: EPS consensus ~$0.76 (2026) vs yfinance forward EPS $1.58, and the EBITDA consensus basis is suspect $MOS. Valuation upside is therefore low-confidence.
