# Nutrient market cycle (MOS quick scan)

**Bundle**: 01 — nutrient-market-cycle
**Parent question**: MOS (The Mosaic Company), quick scan, long-term investor. M1: pricing set by supply discipline, cyclical, returns hinge on supply additions. M2: operational underperformance is fixable self-help.

## Questions addressed
- Current potash/phosphate prices, affordability, demand outlook 2026-27
- Supply side: BHP Jansen timing, China export policy, Russia/Belarus, new capacity
- Multi-year price path views

## Findings

### 1. Prices, affordability, demand (as of Oct 2026)
- **Potash: strong.** Mosaic guides Q3-26 MOP at $270-290/t mine-gate on 2.0-2.2 Mt; summer fill fully subscribed; NA inventories tightening, Brazil restocking, China H1 imports a record ([Mosaic Q2-26 release, 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001285785/000128578526000109/pressreleaseq22026-ex991.htm)). BHP cites FY26 spot +23% to US$342/t Vancouver FOB and early settlement of the 2026 China contract ([BHP 20-F FY26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000811809/000119312526354647/bhp-20260630.htm), via search snippet; not read in full). Nutrien sees 2026 global shipments of 74-77 Mt with "limited new capacity" ([S&P/Nutrien coverage](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/fertilizers/110625-nutrien-increases-2025-potash-sales-guidance-sees-strong-demand-outlook), snippet). Potash is the most affordable nutrient. Medium-high conviction.
- **Phosphate: price high, volume/cost squeezed.** DAP guided $820-840/t Q3. But sulfur (~$705/lt Q3 contract) and ammonia ($610-620/t) are elevated; Mosaic has Faustina fully idled and Bartow at ~40%, with phosphate output constrained through H2-26 and normalization timing "uncertain" ([Mosaic Q2-26 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001285785/000128578526000109/pressreleaseq22026-ex991.htm)). Americas buying picking up as crop prices rise; India Rabi demand strong (same source). Affordability at $820+ DAP is the open risk; no affordability index found in the budget. Low-medium conviction on affordability.
- Note: the Mosaic release is partly a WebFetch summary; verify raw-material figures against the primary PDF before relying on them.

### 2. Supply side
- **BHP Jansen:** Stage 1 first production mid-CY27, 4.2 Mtpa (~5% of global supply); Stage 2 capex raised $4.9B to $6.9B, first production late-FY31 ([BHP FY26 filings via search](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000811809/000119312526354647/bhp-20260630.htm); [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/web/bhps-fertilizer-boss-bets-on-low-cost-potash-to-challenge-rivals/)). Delay plus cost inflation is supportive for incumbents through 2027, with a ramp thereafter. Medium conviction (secondary snippets).
- **China:** phosphate export curbs (DAP/MAP/some NPK) began mid-March 2026 after the Strait of Hormuz crisis and are extended at least through August 2026; Reuters analysis cited as 50-80% of Chinese fertilizer exports restricted ([FertilizerDaily, 2026-07-29](https://www.fertilizerdaily.com/20260729-china-phosphate-export-restrictions-august-2026-russia-quotas/)). Earlier rumor: ~4 Mt DAP exports vs. usual 5-10 Mt ([Datamar](https://datamarnews.com/noticias/chinas-export-controls-drive-up-global-fertilizer-prices/)). Status after August 2026 unknown. China is a key LFP-battery phosphate consumer, so a structural export-limit regime is plausible. Medium conviction.
- **Russia/Belarus:** Russia keeps quantitative fertilizer export limits; EU tariffs on Russian/Belarusian fertilizer step up through 2028; Belarus potash remains sanctioned in the West ([FertilizerDaily](https://www.fertilizerdaily.com/20260729-china-phosphate-export-restrictions-august-2026-russia-quotas/); [Datamar](https://datamarnews.com/noticias/chinas-export-controls-drive-up-global-fertilizer-prices/)). Belarus/Russia still reach China/India/Brazil, so the sanctions are a limited supply constraint. A sanctions thaw is the key downside tail for potash (not researched within budget).
- **Other new capacity:** not covered within budget (K+S Bethune, Laos, Russian EuroChem/Uralkali ramp, OCP phosphate expansion, Ma'aden). Flagged unknown.

### 3. Multi-year price path views
- Jefferies: fertilizer tightness likely persists into 2027 ([StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/General+News/Jefferies%3A+%27Fertilizer+Tightness+Likely+Persists+Into+2027%27/26459148.html), snippet only). World Bank (an older 2025 outlook) expected the fertilizer index to ease in 2026-27 on new capacity ([World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/fertilizer-markets-soften-but-remain-constrained-by-trade-polici)); the 2026 spike (Hormuz, China curbs) has since overtaken that view.
- My synthesis (low-medium conviction): potash holds firm through 2027, then Jansen Stage 1 (+4.2 Mt, 2027-28) plus Russian/Belarusian/Laos growth set up softening around 2028-29 unless demand grows >2%/yr. Phosphate prices are policy-driven (China, Russia) and cost-driven (sulfur/ammonia from Hormuz disruption); both can reverse quickly. Supports M1: this is a cyclical, and today is near a high-price part of the cycle. Normalized-earnings arguments (M2) should be tested against mid-cycle, not current, prices, since current prices flatter potash and the phosphate margin is squeezed by input costs.
