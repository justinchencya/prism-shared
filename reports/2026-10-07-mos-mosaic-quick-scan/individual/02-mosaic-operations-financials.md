# Mosaic operations and financials (Q2 2026)

**Bundle**: 02 — mosaic-operations-financials
**Parent question**: MOS (The Mosaic Company), quick-mode scan, long-term investor. Meta-framings: M1 supply-discipline cyclical; M2 fixable self-help, normalized earnings underpriced.

## Questions addressed
- Latest quarter (Q2 2026) results and guidance: volumes, costs, phosphate production vs targets
- Mosaic Fertilizantes (Brazil), balance sheet/debt/FCF, buybacks/dividend
- Management targets and recent news (asset sales, leadership, litigation, tariffs/duties)

Survey depth, about 6 search/fetch operations. Several figures come from secondary summaries of the call and 8-K (the press-release PDF was unreadable), so treat them as medium confidence.

## Findings

### Q2 2026 results and guidance
- Net sales were $2.8B (vs $3.0B a year earlier). The net loss was $272.8M (-$0.86/share). Adjusted EBITDA was $407M vs $566M a year earlier. Adjusted EPS was $0.13 ([8-K summary, StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/MOS/8-k.html); [Fool transcript summary, 2026-08-11](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/mosaic-mos-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [Indian Chemical News](https://www.indianchemicalnews.com/fertilizer/lite/mosaic-posts-273-million-second-quarter-loss-as-market-pressures-weigh-on-fertilizer-giant-31424)).
- Potash was the profit center. Adjusted EBITDA was $278M on 2.0Mt sold (down from 2.3Mt a year earlier). Phosphate and Fertilizantes both posted operating losses ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/MOS/8-k.html)).
- Phosphate sold 1.4Mt (vs 1.5Mt a year earlier). Mosaic deliberately curtailed production because sulfur and ammonia costs are high and margins are weak. It says it can return to full rates when markets improve ([Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/mosaic-mos-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)).
- Management blames the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and a Kazakhstan blockade for disrupted sulfur flows. It calls spot sulfur uneconomic for the industry.
- Q3 guidance:
  - Phosphate sales of 1.1–1.4Mt at DAP FOB $820–840/t.
  - Realized sulfur cost of about $700–710/t and ammonia of about $610–620/t.
  - Potash sales of 2.0–2.2Mt at $270–290/t.
  - Q3 sulfur was contracted at about $705/long ton, below spot.
  - Working-capital release of $300–500M, mostly in Q4.
- FY2026 targets: potash production of 9.0Mt, capex cut to $1.2B (from $1.25B), SG&A of $510–530M ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/MOS/8-k.html)).
- Phosphate production versus the prior ~2Mt/quarter run-rate target: the current phosphate cost squeeze is a purchased-input problem (sulfur, ammonia), not an operating problem. Because of the curtailment, the self-help metric (production rate) is unmeasurable right now. I could not find a stated quarterly rate figure in the sources read. Treat this as unknown.
- Management says global phosphate production is expected to fall about 30Mt below the prior year (Fool summary). This is supportive for M1, because supply is being cut on cost rather than by discipline.

### Mosaic Fertilizantes (Brazil), balance sheet, capital returns
- Fertilizantes sold 1.5Mt (vs 2.2Mt a year earlier). Reported EBITDA was about $60M per the call summary, but the 8-K summary describes an operating loss. These are not necessarily inconsistent (EBITDA vs operating income), but I have not reconciled them ([Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/mosaic-mos-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)).
- Restructuring:
  - In March 2026 Mosaic committed to divest the Araxá mining and chemical complex and idle the Patrocínio mines. This produced a charge of about $442M in Q1 and about $40M in Q2 ([10-Q Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001285785/000128578526000112/mos-20260630.htm), via search snippet).
  - Mosaic also granted Vale an option to buy its minority stakes in Fosfertil and Fertifos and its Cubatão operations, at a price above $1B (same 10-Q).
- Cash flow: Q2 operating cash flow was $167M and free cash flow was -$153M (StockTitan). Mosaic completed the Carlsbad potash mine sale and arranged a $1B term loan.
- Debt actions: a debt tender offer dated 2026-08-10 is being funded by a new notes issuance ([424B5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001285785/000119312526347026/d292796d424b5.htm), via snippet). This looks like liability management and refinancing.
- Net debt and leverage: unknown. I did not retrieve the figures. This is the main gap.
- Dividend: the quarterly dividend of $0.22/share was paid and held (StockTitan). I found no evidence of active buybacks. FCF was negative, so buybacks look unlikely near term (low-to-medium conviction).

### Management targets and news
- Leadership: Bruce Bodine remains President and CEO. I found no leadership change.
- Asset-sale program: Carlsbad sold, Araxá in progress, Vale option on the Brazilian stakes. This is portfolio pruning toward potash and the core phosphate system.
- Mosaic Biosciences revenue is projected to double 2025's $68M (Fool summary). It is small relative to the company.
- Trade: the U.S. Court of International Trade sustained Commerce's remand on the Morocco CVD review on 2025-12-16 ([CIT 25-03 / search result](https://www.cit.uscourts.gov/sites/cit/files/25-03.pdf)). Commerce planned a March 2026 review of whether to end the Morocco/Russia phosphate duty orders ([Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/de/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2780056-us-to-review-russia-morocco-phos-duties-in-march)). I did not find the outcome of that review. A removal of the orders would be a downside risk to U.S. phosphate pricing.

## Read-through to the meta-framings
- M1 (supply discipline): partly supported. DAP is guided at $820–840/t, but margins are crushed by input costs. Supply is shrinking because of costs and geopolitics, not producer discipline. Pricing is vulnerable once sulfur normalizes and curtailed capacity restarts.
- M2 (fixable self-help): inconclusive. The Q2 data is dominated by exogenous sulfur/ammonia costs. The self-help evidence is the Brazil restructuring and asset sales, not a recovery in production rates.
